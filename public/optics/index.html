A Brief History
of Optics.

A small map of the tools researchers use to look inside language models, from simple probes to the stranger lenses of 2026.

I. Timeline ↓II. Family tree ↓III. Instrument cabinet ↓

When a language model processes a sentence, it passes around enormous lists of numbers. Researchers call them activations. You can’t just glance at one and see what it means, so people have built tools to help us look.

Some tools turn those numbers into possible next words. Others ask a model for an explanation, find recurring features, or trace how those features affect an answer. These are different views, not photographs of what a model is thinking. Here are some of the inventions and how they connect.

This is a starting map, not a complete history. The lines show a few useful relationships; methods that sit nearby weren’t necessarily built from one another. Click around, follow a paper, and tell me if I missed something.
